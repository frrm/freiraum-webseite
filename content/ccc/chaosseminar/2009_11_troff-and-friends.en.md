Almost every computer enthusiast knows and uses TeX when creating documents. But there are other typesetting systems too, which are no worse — just less well known. One is troff, from Bell Labs, closely intertwined with the creation of Unix.

Today, troff is primarily used to format man pages. Yet a remarkable number of computer books have also been typeset with troff, showing that it is serious software for professional use. Nevertheless, troff is now a little dusty and hardly known. This talk will sweep away the dust and show why troff is worth exploring.

Starting with the historical background, the talk will explain troff's design. It will introduce how to use troff itself and the best-known macro packages that make it easier to use. Various preprocessors will also be demonstrated and explained. These make it easy, for example, to produce mathematical formulas or diagrams.

A large part of the talk will be a live demonstration of the programs. Audience participation is expressly encouraged.

Starring: troff, nroff, tbl, eqn, refer, pic
