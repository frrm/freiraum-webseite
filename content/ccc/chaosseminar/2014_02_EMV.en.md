Or — how Marcus zapped microcontrollers in the lab.

All electrically powered devices generate electromagnetic fields to varying degrees, and can themselves be affected by fields above a certain intensity. This may be intentional (for example, in wireless communication), but can of course also cause unwanted effects such as malfunctions or complete device failure. National and international electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) guidelines define the field intensities electronic devices must tolerate without malfunctioning.

The talk begins with a brief look at the electrical-engineering fundamentals needed to illustrate how electromagnetic fields arise and what effects they have. Key topics include the transition from ideal to real components as frequency increases, the effects of changing spatial dimensions, and ways devices can influence one another through (unintentional) coupling. Each aspect is illustrated with familiar real-world examples, such as why the cable from a USB mouse is not suitable for connecting a USB hard drive.

At the beginning of the seminar there will be a short update on acquiring new hardware for our “dontpanic”.
