Bitcoin is the first digital currency without central control. Instead, a distributed database tracks all participants’ balances and creates new money at a predetermined rate. The technical trick that makes such a system possible is requiring proof of work to change the global state of the distributed system. This mainly prevents bitcoins from being spent more than once and balances from being forged — but only as long as more than 50% of the computing power in the distributed system acts honestly.

This talk explains the technical details of the Bitcoin system and what they mean for participants and operators. Macroeconomic topics and the everyday war over money will only be touched on, as I am not an expert in them.
