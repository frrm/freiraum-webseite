Patents are intended to protect inventors, reward their research and development efforts, and give them room to turn a new idea into a product by granting a monopoly. Critics, however, suspect that in practice they have become yet another instrument of power for large corporations and their lawyers.

EU bodies are now working on a change to the law on patent protection for algorithms and computer programs. The possible introduction of so-called software patents in the EU raises many questions.

What could then be patented, and why was this not possible before? What consequences would this have for me personally, for small businesses, the open-source community, and software development in general?

In this talk, guest speaker Christian Cremer will answer these and other questions from the perspective of a patent attorney and shed light on the background and laws of patent law. Afterwards, we can expect a lively discussion of this often controversial subject.
