**Announcement in English:**

Forced by the US, Europe is just about to adopt a new form of patents that were illegal up to now: Patents on Software. Whereas most big companies support the idea of software patents, most small and medium sized businesses as well as most open source developers see them as a serious threat to their profession.

Richard Stallman will explain how software patents obstruct software development. Software patents are patents that cover software ideas. They restrict the development of software, so that every design decision brings a risk of getting sued. Patents in other fields restrict factories, but software patents restrict every computer user. Economic research shows that they even retard progress.

About the lecturer:
Richard Stallman is the founder of the GNU Project, launched in 1984 to develop the free software operating system GNU. He is the principal author of the GNU Compiler Collection (gcc), the GNU symbolic debugger (gdb), GNU Emacs, and various other programs for the GNU operating system.

[Stallman](http://stallman.org/) graduated from Harvard in 1974 with a BA in physics. During his college years, he also worked as a staff hacker at the MIT Artificial Intelligence Lab, learning operating system development by doing it. In January 1984 he resigned from MIT to start the GNU project. Today he is one of the leaders of the free software movement and president of the Free Software Foundation (FSF).

**Announcement in German:**

At the urging of the United States, the European Union is currently working on introducing a new form of patent that has not previously been lawful: patents on software and logic. While large corporations in particular support software patents, many small and medium-sized software companies and open source developers see them as a threat to their livelihoods.

In his talk, Richard Stallman explains how software patents hinder software development, since every line of code then carries the risk of being sued by a patent holder. Until now, patents mainly affected large companies; now every computer user is affected directly or indirectly. Scientific studies even show that software patents impede progress.

About the speaker:
[Richard Stallman](http://www.stallman.org/) founded the GNU Project, which was launched in 1984 with the goal of developing the free GNU operating system. He is the principal author of the GNU Compiler Collection (gcc), the GNU debugger (gdb), the GNU Emacs editor, and many other programs. Stallman completed his physics degree at Harvard in 1974 and then worked as a programmer at the MIT Artificial Intelligence Lab, where he learned operating system development through practical work. In 1984, he left MIT to launch the GNU Project. Today Stallman is considered one of the pioneers and leaders of the free software community. He is president of the Free Software Foundation (FSF).
