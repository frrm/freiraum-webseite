The topic, explained at length: this consists of two talks.

The first brings together stories of mistranslations and amusing misunderstandings experienced by Marcus and his language students. It is intended as a light-hearted introduction to possible ways of thinking in foreign languages. The talk will be in German. The languages touched on include German, English, Portuguese, Russian, and Polish, and — depending on how well the contributors do their homework — perhaps also Finnish and Turkish, very roughly.

The second part dives into writing systems and Unicode. The talk is not yet fully outlined, but will cover endianness, encodings, the Unicode Consortium, and parts of the code pages.

A few questions to help you orient yourself:

- How does `lowercase()` work for alphabets with different numbers of letters?
- What is alphabetical sorting when my music collection also contains Russian artists?
- How do I compare two strings?
- The Compose key.

Have fun, and see you Monday!

Marcus

### Addendum
The **social website** is [polyglotclub.com](http://polyglotclub.com); users create profiles describing the languages they know and the language they want to learn. They can submit texts for correction by the community. The website works fine functionally, but has implementation problems.

**Books**: For auditory learners, *Assimil* is recommended. A small book presents selected texts in increasing difficulty, read slowly by native speakers. The translation is on the opposite page. Grammar lessons appear at regular intervals. Polish native speakers’ assessment: although the early texts contain grammatically funny constructions (“which is kind of funny”), they are correct. The problem disappears as the difficulty increases (“in the middle of the book”). *Kauderwelsch* follows the 80/20 rule. Its double translations are interesting: (1) word for word and (2) grammatically natural German. The quality of Kauderwelsch varies by language (multiple authors; thanks, maha! Esperanto is great; Portuguese in an indefinably old edition is not so praiseworthy).
