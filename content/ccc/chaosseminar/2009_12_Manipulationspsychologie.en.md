In good security systems, technical measures are not the only decisive factor. On closer inspection, a security strategy always includes people too (for example, the administrator). Attacks on this human component therefore deserve our full attention as well.

For good reason, so-called "social engineering," which targets precisely these "security vulnerabilities," has a long tradition among hackers. "Hacking" need not be limited to devices. It can also mean putting sophisticated psychology to use.

This talk is not limited to social engineering, however. It aims to show that we are practically surrounded by manipulative psychology. The advertising industry, intelligence agencies, cults, and fraudsters in particular make use of all kinds of psychological findings.
