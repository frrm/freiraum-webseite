In the 1980s, Logo was *the* educational programming language. People preferred BASIC, Fortran, or Turbo Pascal for writing practical programs, but Logo drew beautiful pictures. One thing inseparably associated with Logo is so-called “turtle graphics.”

This talk gives a brief overview of 2D, 3D, and 4D turtle graphics, along with some fun extensions. Topics include drawing with a compass and straightedge, turtle image processing, and fractals.

If time permits, the second part of the talk will introduce programming languages inspired by Logo that were designed for and are suitable for children. The tensions between “designed for children” and “suitable for children,” “suitable for children” and “suitable for teachers,” and “interesting for children” and “useful for adults” should provide material for an interesting discussion.
