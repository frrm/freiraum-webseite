Almost all current operating-system distributions are released on CDs or DVDs with binary packages, often accompanied by many pages of documentation. The Linux From Scratch project, which grew out of a HOWTO, instead gives users who want to install an operating system only a handbook. It explains step by step how to compile and install the kernel and basic programs from source code.

The talk surveys the history and build methods of Linux From Scratch since its first version in 1999. It shows how to construct a working toolchain — the heart of an LFS system — despite circular package dependencies. Technical details are not neglected as we look under the hood of this non-distribution, whose slogan is “Your distro. Your rules.” and whose goal is to teach what happens inside a Linux system.
